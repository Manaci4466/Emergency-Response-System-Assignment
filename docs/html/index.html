Emergency Response Decision-Support System

Artificial Intelligence, UAntwerpen. Report: `report.md` · LLM prompt log: PROMPTS.md

The system plans which emergency responder goes to which incident, and along which route, in a city whose road conditions are uncertain. It combines the course techniques in one pipeline (Notes N = the course notes, UC Berkeley CS 188 Note N):

  1. Bayesian network (Notes 6): noisy road reports (citizen, police, sensor) + weather → P(road status).
  2. Hidden Markov model (Notes 8): historical traffic observations → belief over the current traffic level.
  3. A* search (Notes 1): fastest route on the directed road graph with expected travel times.
  4. Constraint satisfaction (Notes 2): responders are assigned to incidents with backtracking, MRV, forward checking and branch and bound, round by round as incidents become known.

run.py writes the plan (plan.json) and its statistics (statistics.txt), and an independent validator re-checks the written plan.

Option Default Meaning

4. Output files

**plan.json**: a list of assignments with exactly the four keys from the assignment, sorted by time and then responder id. time is the simulation time (s) at which the responder starts the job.

[
{"responder_id": "R1", "incident_id": "I2", "path": ["N1", "H1", "N2"], "time": 0.0},
{"responder_id": "R2", "incident_id": "I4", "path": ["H1", "N2"], "time": 0.0}
]

**statistics.txt** starts with the four required lines (small, CSP):

System statistics:
* 7 incidents handled
* 4 responders used
* Average response time: 112.8 s
* Deadlines met: 28.6%

followed by details (on time, Critical on time, priority-weighted missed, per-priority breakdown, late and unhandled incidents, the route-search heuristic used, node-limit warnings) and a "Definitions" block:

  • Units: seconds; all times are absolute simulation times from t = 0.
  • Incidents handled: incidents with an assigned responder.
  • Responders used: distinct responders with at least one assignment.
  • Average response time: mean of (arrival − incident timestamp) over handled incidents only.
  • Deadlines met: on-time arrivals (arrival ≤ deadline) / all incidents; unhandled = missed.
  • Priority-weighted missed: Σ priority weights (Critical 8, High 4, Medium 2, Low 1) of late or unhandled incidents.
  • All times are expected times under the probabilistic model, not observed ground truth.

5. How it works

observed_traffic ────────► hmm.py ───► P(Low / Medium / High) per directed road ────────┤
▼
cost_model.py: expected travel time per road (None if P(Blocked) ≥ tau)
▼
incidents (by timestamp) ► simulator.py ─► allocation.py (CSP per round) ─► search.py (A*)
▼
run.py ─► plan.json + statistics.txt ─► validator.py
Module What it does Course link
src/data_loader.py reads one scenario into typed dataclasses; checks probability rows, ids, weather consistency, observation numbering and value names at load time –
src/bayes.py Weather → Status → {Citizen, Police, Sensor}; P(S | w, reports) ∝ P(S|w) · Π P(report|S) by enumeration; weather summed out for unreported roads Notes 6
src/hmm.py forward algorithm (time elapse, observe, normalise), one prediction step to t = 0, stationary distribution Notes 8
src/cost_model.py time = distance/speed × status multiplier × traffic multiplier; unusable if P(Blocked) ≥ tau –
src/search.py A* graph search with a closed set, h = straight-line distance / fastest road speed; falls back to uniform-cost search if a road is shorter than the straight line Notes 1
src/allocation.py per-round CSP: backtracking, MRV, forward checking on deadlines, branch and bound on (priority-weighted late, total response time); greedy baseline Notes 2
src/simulator.py rounds by incident timestamp, no clairvoyance, assignments committed immediately –
src/statistics.py the statistics and the statistics.txt text –
src/validator.py independent plan check: reads only plan.json + raw files (never the planner's search or allocation code) –
src/experiments.py sensitivity analyses and Monte Carlo robustness Notes 6 (prior sampling)
tools/mutation_sweep.py injects 35 known bugs, one at a time, and checks the tests catch each one –

Design decisions and assumptions

  1. A road is unusable if P(Blocked) ≥ tau (0.5); otherwise the status multiplier is (P(O)·1 + P(C)·1.5) / (P(O) + P(C)), the expected slowdown given that the road is passable.
  2. Roads without reports get the prior marginal over weather: Open 0.685, Congested 0.18, Blocked 0.135 (multiplier ≈ 1.104).
  3. Traffic "now" = filtered HMM belief + one prediction step; road states are fixed for the whole run (all evidence is available at t = 0).
  4. Traffic multipliers Low 1.0, Medium 1.25, High 1.5; the expected value over the belief is multiplied with the status multiplier.
  5. Roads without traffic observations get traffic multiplier 1.0.
  6. Roads are directed: A→B and B→A never share reports or observations.
  7. Assignments are committed immediately (no re-planning when a later incident arrives).
  8. Busy responders can be queued; after a job a responder waits at the incident location.
  9. Deadlines are soft: late help is better than no help.
  10. Response time = arrival − incident timestamp.

Additional safeguards: the A* heuristic uses the fastest road speed of the scenario and is replaced by h = 0 (uniform-cost search, with a warning) if any road is shorter than the straight line between its ends, because then h could overestimate. If the CSP hits its node limit before finding any complete plan for a round, that round falls back to the greedy plan, so no incident is silently dropped.

6. Testing

python -m pytest # 110 passed, 1 skipped
RUN_MUTATION_SWEEP=1 python -m pytest tests/test_mutation_sweep.py -s # the slow overarching check
python tools/mutation_sweep.py # the same, as a script
  • Hand-calculated values: e.g. the Bayes posterior for small N1→N2 (0.014 / 0.459 / 0.527), the classic umbrella HMM exactly with fractions (2/5 and likelihood 15/128), the stationary traffic distribution (45, 49, 33)/127, the small t = 0 allocation (late weight 12 vs greedy 16).
  • Differential testing: A* = uniform-cost search = an independent Floyd–Warshall in the test, on every node pair; the CSP = exhaustive brute force on all three t = 0 rounds.
  • Properties: the heuristic is consistent for every goal, tau sets are nested, no clairvoyance (changing a future incident never changes an earlier assignment).
  • Validator fault injection: 13 injected plan faults, each caught with exactly the expected error.
  • Mutation sweep (tools/mutation_sweep.py): 35 typical bugs across all modules (flipped CPTs, off-by-one in the HMM, an inadmissible heuristic, no MRV, ignoring busy responders, clairvoyance, wrong statistics denominators, a validator that forgets checks, ...). Each is applied to a temporary copy of the project and the whole suite is run: 35 of 35 caught.

7. Results

Committed runs (tau 0.5, default options) are in results/<scenario>-<policy>/; compare a fresh run with diff output/small/plan.json results/small-csp/plan.json. All times are expected seconds.

Scenario Policy Handled Responders used Avg response (s) Deadlines met Priority-weighted missed Critical on time
1 (small) CSP 7/7 4 112.8 28.6% 24 1/2
1 (small) greedy 7/7 4 112.2 28.6% 28 0/2
2 (medium) CSP 12/12 8 34.6 91.7% 4 6/6
2 (medium) greedy 12/12 8 37.2 91.7% 8 5/6
3 (large) CSP 20/20 8 1319.2 65.0% 36 5/8
3 (large) greedy 20/20 8 1319.2 65.0% 36 5/8

The unweighted deadline percentage is the same for both policies, but the CSP keeps a Critical incident on time where greedy does not (small and medium). Small misses several deadlines by only 1–5 s.

results/experiments.md (regenerate with python -m src.experiments) contains:

  • tau 0.1 / 0.5 / 0.6 / 0.9: 0.1 closes every unreported road and disconnects the maps; 0.6 opens the roads at P(Blocked) = 0.527; 0.9 reconnects medium, whose two halves are linked only by N1↔N2 (P(Blocked) = 0.83).
  • Assumptions: treating unreported roads as open lifts small from 28.6% to 85.7%; the stationary traffic multiplier for unobserved roads (×1.25 in medium) drops medium from 91.7% to 33.3%.
  • Monte Carlo (5000 sampled worlds, seed 2026): true road states and traffic levels are sampled from the beliefs and the fixed plan is replayed. At least one planned route is blocked in 68% / 64% / 97% of worlds (small / medium / large). R1's route to the Critical I2 in small is blocked in 24.9% of worlds (analytically 1 − 0.865² = 25.2%). Raising tau to 0.6 doubles small's planned deadlines met (28.6% → 57.1%) but not the sampled one (36.2% → 35.1%).

8. Limitations

  • All statistics are expected values under the model; a "valid" plan is valid under the believed road states, not the true ones (see the Monte Carlo results).
  • Road states and traffic are estimated once at t = 0 and used for the whole simulation.
  • Assignments are never re-planned when a more urgent incident arrives later.
  • The CSP is optimal within one round, but the job order of a shared responder follows the search order, so it is not exhaustive over all job orders (brute force agrees on all three t = 0 rounds).
  • Reports are assumed conditionally independent given the road status; the traffic and status multipliers are chosen, not learned.
